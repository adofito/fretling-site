Say it in plain language

One sentence runs across the top of the screen, and every colored word in it is a dropdown. Change a word and the neck redraws.

  • Learn or identify — the intent you are in.
  • Scales, chords or triads — what you are looking at.
  • Any root, and twelve scales, four triad types, or the chords of the key.

There is no theory jargon at the front door. Major, Minor and both Pentatonics lead the scale menu. More scales holds Blues, which is free too, and seven advanced scales that need the unlock. Pro

See the whole neck

A scale is drawn across every string and every fret at once, not one box at a time. Chords arrive with real fingerings from a built-in database. Triads are grouped by string set, so you can walk one shape up the neck.

Below the neck sit the overlays: CAGED, Box Shapes and 3NPS. Switch one on and it draws over what is already there. CAGED grips follow the chord, so a major chord gets major grips and a minor chord gets minor ones.

Think in intervals

One color per interval, everywhere in the app: the same pitch is the same color on the neck, on the timeline and in the Chord Atlas. Hue advances a fixed step per semitone, and three lightness bands keep neighbors apart for a reader who cannot separate red from green.

  • RRootUnison
  • ♭3Minor 3rdThree semitones
  • 3Major 3rdFour semitones
  • 5Perfect 5thSeven semitones
  • ♭7Minor 7thTen semitones
  • 7Major 7thEleven semitones

Shapes = Intervals gives every degree its own outline as well as its own color, so the neck still reads when you switch color off. More about the fretboard

Play it, name it

Tap the notes you are fingering onto the neck and Fretling names the chord, gives its Roman numeral in your key, says why, and lists the alternate readings underneath. The spelling strip shows every note in the chord, with the lowest badged as the bass.

Or play it, and let Fretling listen instead. Pro

How identifying works

Every chord, in context

The Chord Atlas opens from the header. Pick a root, pick a quality, hear it before you commit, and watch it land on the neck.

The Atlas knows the key you are in. It marks each chord with its Roman numeral, and when you pick one from outside the key it says where the chord was borrowed from, rather than leaving you to work out why something that is not in the key still sounds right.

Free, in every mode. More about the Chord Atlas

Hear what you see

Every marker is a button. Tap it to hear that note; press and hold to sustain it. On iPad you can hold several at once and slide along a string.

The scale timeline under the neck lays the key out left to right. Press the transport at the head of a rail to run the scale, or to sustain the chord, and the line beneath spells out what you are about to hear.

The voices are sampled instruments, not beeps: a grand piano, nylon and steel acoustic guitars, jazz and clean electric guitars, and a sine wave when you want the pitch and nothing else. Chords can stack up to three pad voices on top.

Plug in your guitar Pro

Monitor your own playing through a Neural Amp Modeler profile. Five models are built in (two clean, crunch, warm and acoustic), and you can load any .nam file you already own.

One input console serves the amp, the tuner and Live Detect together: interface, channel, gain, buffer size and sample rate in one place, with the round-trip latency shown in milliseconds.

The looper records what you play over the click, with bars, count-in, overdubs and undo, so a progression you are working on becomes something to solo over.

On iPad, use a USB-C interface or headphones. Monitoring through the built-in speaker feeds straight back into the microphone, and the amp says so rather than letting it howl.

Practice tools, built like gear

Six panels float over the neck — these five and the amp above. Each one drags where you want it, folds to a one-row strip that keeps working, and remembers where you left it.

  • Tuner — chromatic, to within 5 cents, free. Precision tuning to half a cent, with the Trace, Strobe and Disc displays, is Pro
  • Metronome — 40 to 250 BPM, eleven meters, five subdivisions, tap tempo and three click sounds, all free.
  • Drums — a recorded acoustic kit with 61 beats in four sections and a step sequencer to edit them. Pro
  • Mixer — a fader for every voice: notes, chords, the click, the amp and the loop.
  • Live Detect — Fretling listens and names what you are playing as you play it. Pro

Two finishes, Machined and Studio, or let them follow the theme. The space bar starts and stops the click and the loop together. More about the practice tools

Make it yours

A bar above the neck carries the four decisions you change most often: Labels (note names, interval numbers or nothing), Shapes, Colors and Accidentals. They are chips beside the fretboard, not settings buried in a sidebar.

  • Show 12 to 22 frets, and slide a position window along the board.
  • Light and dark, following the system or pinned.
  • 14 app icons.
  • English and Latin American Spanish throughout, including this site.

Private by default

Fretling runs entirely on your device. There is no account to create, no analytics, no tracking and no cloud sync — what you set up stays on the Mac or iPad you set it up on.

The microphone is used by exactly three things: the tuner, Live Detect and the amp. Audio is analyzed as it arrives and is never recorded, stored or sent anywhere, and the microphone is only open while one of those is. The privacy policy
