The path

The home is one path: the eight modes on a line, a square for each. The mode you are on is open, with its level, a sentence saying what its rounds ask, a small picture of the lesson and Start. A mode opens the next one once you pass its first level. On a Mac and a wide iPad the path is the sidebar, and the mode you pick fills the window; on a Mac the up and down arrows walk the path. Free practice, Practice and Settings open from the home: on an iPhone from its toolbar, and on a Mac and a wide iPad Free practice sits at the foot of the path and Practice is the row under the last mode.

A round: Hear, Check, Sing

Every round runs the same three steps, shown across the top. Hear plays it with its names. Check asks what you heard; the answers come up once it has played. Sing asks you to sing the note back. The marks under the round count the right answers toward the level.

The eight modes

In the path's order, each with the question its rounds ask. Higher or lower, Intervals and Chord quality are free at every level; every other mode's first level is free, and Pro opens the rest. Pro

  • Higher or lower. Two notes, one after the other. Is the second higher or lower?
  • Intervals. Two notes, one after the other or together. Which interval is it?
  • Chord quality. A chord, one note at a time and then together. What kind of chord is it?
  • Spotlight. A held chord brings one of its voices forward. Which one is it?
  • Missing tone. A held chord loses one of its voices. Which one went missing?
  • Scales and modes. A scale up and back down over its tonic. Which scale or mode is it?
  • Scale degrees. A cadence or the drone sets the key, then one note. Which degree is it?
  • Chords in context. A key, then one of its chords with one voice brought forward. Which degree is it?

Spotlight and the help level

Spotlight's levels climb from open triads to close and inverted chords to 7th chords, and inside each the help fades: a solo and a boost, a boost, a light boost, then none. On Voice lanes the Help level row (Solo, Boost, Light, None) lets you pick more help for a round; a round with more help than its level gives doesn't count toward passing. Pro

Sing, your range and headphones

The first time you sing, Earling asks for your voice range, once. The right note in any octave that suits you counts, held for about half a second, closer as the levels climb. Headphones help: on speakers the chord stops while you sing, so Earling hears only you; with headphones it keeps playing under your voice (on a Mac, turn on I’m using headphones in Settings). Without the microphone, Sing becomes a tap (I sang it). Settings › Singing › Sing after each answer turns Sing off for quiet practice, and the microphone never opens.

The drone and the cadence

Turn on the drone, a soft tonic under the round: in Missing tone, Scale degrees and Chords in context it turns the answers into degrees of the key. A short I–IV–V–I cadence can set the key before the chord. Both are free, and Settings › Sound holds the chord sound, the drone and its volume.

Three structure views

The Pitch stack stands a chord on one rule, its voices as squares, the one you are hearing run out as a bar. The Pitch wheel places the voices on a ring of the twelve notes. Voice lanes gives each voice a lane across the window. The iPhone and iPad open on the stack and the Mac on the lanes; Settings › Appearance › Structure view picks any of the three.

Free practice

The pad holds a chord you pick, in any key, voicing and inversion; tap a voice to bring it forward. Below it, every mode and its levels, to play any level you have opened without moving the path.

Your practice

Practice shows this week, then time and right answers by mode over the last eight weeks, against the 80% a level asks. Your progress, your voice range and this log stay on the device.

On a Mac: the keys

Up, down and = answer Higher or lower; 1, 3, 5 and 7 name a chord’s voices, and a degree answers by its number. Space or ⌘R plays it again, ⌘. pauses, and D turns the drone on or off. Return presses the round’s main button: Start, Next or I sang it.
