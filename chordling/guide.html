Guide and Support

Chordling keeps one song on one stage: the melody in the tall lane at the top, the chords under it as labels that say what each one does, a row between them that says why each chord works, and every other part at the foot. The switch at the top picks what you’re doing: Write the chords and the melody, Harmonize to try chords for your melody, Arrange to set each part’s sound and level, the song’s sections and the band. This guide walks through each, then covers settings, exporting and getting help.

For Chordling 1.0 on Mac, iPad and iPhone. Menu commands are the Mac’s; on iPad and iPhone the same actions are on the lanes and in the top bar’s More menu (⋯).

Songs

Songs, at the left of the top bar, opens the song list, where every song lives. New Song opens a sheet with two ways in: Start with chords or Start with a melody. Start from Demo opens one of 20 demo songs, from a first scale to advanced harmony. Right-click or long-press a song to Rename or Delete it; deleting can’t be undone. There’s no limit on how many songs you keep.

The melody

The melody has its own lane, a piano roll at the top of the stage. With the Draw tool, drag across the grid to place a note; the tinted rows are the notes in your key. The Tools menu on the melody lane switches between Draw, Select and Erase, and sets Snap to. To play a tune in instead, turn on Tap keys on the melody lane: a keyboard strip opens under the melody, and each key you tap adds the next note. Undo is in the top bar; Redo is in More.

The chords

The chords sit in their lane under the melody, each one a label in its own color with its numeral and what it does: Home, Away, Pull home and the like. In an empty song the lane offers a Start from menu of ten progressions to begin with, such as Pop I–V–vi–IV, Jazz ii–V–I, the 12-bar blues and the Andalusian cadence; once there’s a melody it offers Chords for this tune too.

For the whole progression at once, tap the Chords lane’s Sound chip and choose Edit Progression… at the foot of its popover. The progression editor shows the chords four ways: Wheel, Map, Ladder and Voices. Its detail panel ends with Under the melody: the chords that contain the note your melody holds where the pick would land, and that note’s role in each.

Color bands and melody guides

Each chord’s color washes down its bars behind the melody, so you can see which notes fit. Color bands (Bands on an iPhone) on the melody lane turns it off or on. For finer guides, open the melody’s Part settings (at the foot of its lane’s popover, or More › Part settings) and choose a level under Melody guides: Scale, Chord Tones, Guide Tones or Targets. The Chords lane’s Part settings add Guides on the canvas, with chord guides and their strength.

Harmonize a melody

Choose Harmonize in the switch at the top (on a Mac also View › Harmonize, ⌘2). Rows A, B and C of chords appear under the song’s chords, and A fits your melody best. The bar above them sets the style: How rich runs from Simple and Sevenths through Colors and Dominants to Jazz; a Flavor (Pedal point, Modal or Minor color) plays in its place; and How often sets how often the chords change (Every bar, Every half bar or Follow the melody). The rows answer as soon as you change any of them.

Tap a chord in any row and that bar takes it into your mix, with why it is there under it; tap a row’s letter to take the whole row. Pick a different row for each bar and press Play to hear your mix in the song’s place. Harmonize again keeps the bars you picked and finds new chords around them. Apply mix (or Apply A for one row whole) writes the chords into your song, in one step you can undo.

Simple and Sevenths are free. Colors, Dominants, Jazz and the three flavors come with Chordling Pro.

Why it works

Why it works is the row between the melody and the chords. Each bar says in two words what its chord does, with the melody’s notes in that bar, filled where they sit inside the chord. Tap a bar, or the row’s name, for the card: what each change does and the cadence it makes, drawn as a map or a wheel. On an iPhone the card is also at the foot of the Chords lane’s popover. Add chords first; it reads whatever the song holds.

Harmony parts

Add a part that harmonizes your melody with + Part, at the end of the Band row at the foot of the stage (on Mac also Harmony › Add Harmony, ⇧⌘H). Add Harmony adds the blend Chordling suggests for your melody, and More blends… lets you pick another: Classic blend (below), Wide blend (below), Blend above, Octave double, Follow the chords or Bass under melody. Empty Part gives you a part to write by hand. Each part gets a chip in the Band row (a slim lane in Arrange) with its sound, level, mute and solo, and its Part settings hold its name, color and blend.

One harmony part is free. A second and third part, and the sung Voice sounds, come with Chordling Pro. A song holds up to three harmony parts.

Listen, Record and Pitch Matching

Listen, in the top bar’s More menu (on a Mac also Playback › Listen), shows the pitch you sing on the melody lane while it’s on. It’s free. With Chordling Pro, each note you sing also gets a mark once the song passes it: a check on pitch, a chevron up or down when sharp or flat, a ring when missed.

Record, beside Listen in the melody lane’s header and in More, has two rows, both with Chordling Pro. Sing Notes into Melody: press Play, sing after the count-in, then Stop, and what you sang replaces the part’s notes in those bars, in one step you can undo. Record a Take: press Play, sing, then Stop, and your voice is saved as a take in the Takes lane, which plays with the song and goes into Export Audio. Use headphones, so Record hears only you.

The Pitch Matching Test (Settings › App › Pitch Matching › Start the Test…) grades how closely you match a sung or piano guide; it comes with Chordling Pro. Only a take keeps what the microphone hears, inside your song: see the privacy policy.

Arrange a song

Arrange is where a written song gets its shape and its sound: what plays in each lane and how loud, the sections the song runs through, and the band behind it. Choose it in the switch at the top (on a Mac also View › Arrange, ⌘3). The lanes stay where they are, but their headers widen to show every chip, and the slim lanes grow taller to hold them. The Form row over the ruler, where the sections live, shows in every mode, so you can lay out sections while you write too.

The lane headers

Each lane’s header names the lane and holds its chips. Click a chip (tap it on iPad and iPhone) to open its popover. The first chip’s popover also holds the lane’s Mix and, at its foot, the lane’s other doors, such as Part settings.

  • Chords: Sound, what plays the chords (the singer-friendly sounds first, the rest under More); Voicing, how they’re voiced (Smooth, Piano or 4-Part), how high they sit (Register) and a left-hand bass, which rests while the band’s bass plays; and Why it works (Why? where the header is narrow).
  • Melody: its tools, as in Write, then its Sound and a level slider, with M and S beside its name. In a shorter window, as on an iPad, the header drops the slider, and the level moves into the Sound popover’s Mix; shorter still, it drops the Sound too, which the melody’s Part settings also hold.
  • Each harmony part: its Sound, with M and S once the part has notes; its level is in the Sound popover’s Mix. Click the part’s name to edit it on the melody lane.
  • Bass and Drums: the band, below. While the drums play, clicking the Drums lane itself opens the drum editor.

Level, mute and solo. The slider sets the lane’s level. M mutes the lane; S solos it, so only soloed lanes play (you can solo several). While the chords or a band lane is muted or soloed, a small M or S shows beside its name. Levels, mutes and solos are kept with the song and aren’t undo steps. On an iPhone each lane shows one chip, which opens everything the lane has.

Sections

Sections are named runs of bars: Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Solo, Outro, or a plain Section you name yourself. They show twice. The Form row over the ruler draws them along the timeline, each block with its name and, when it plays more than once, how many times (×2). The Form panel lists them one under another, which is where you add, reorder, repeat and rename them. Sections are free, and every change to them is one step you can undo; the notes and chords in a section always go with it.

The Form panel

Click Form at the start of the Form row (tap it on iPad and iPhone) to open the panel, where Part settings opens; on a Mac also View › Form, ⌥⌘F. Where the top bar keeps Part settings under More, Form is there too. Close it with its ✕ or Esc.

  • A row is a section: its name, its bars, its repeats (×1 to ×4) and a More menu with everything the section can do: its kind, Rename…, the repeats, Key and Tempo…, Duplicate, Split at Playhead, Move Earlier, Move Later and Delete. While the band’s drums play, three small bars show how hard they play there.
  • Click a row to select the section and move the playhead to its first bar. While the song plays, it carries on from there.
  • Drag a row up or down to move the section (on iPad and iPhone, touch and hold it first). It takes the place of the row you drop it on.
  • Add Section, at the list’s foot, adds four empty bars as a section of the kind you choose, after the selected section, or at the end when none is selected. The Form row’s + always adds them at the end.
  • While the song plays, the playing section’s row is marked, and a section that repeats counts its pass (2/3).

The Form row’s blocks have the same menu: right-click a block (on iPad and iPhone, touch and hold it). With VoiceOver, a row and a block both offer Move Earlier, Move Later, Duplicate, Rename, Key and Tempo and Delete as actions, and swiping up or down on a row changes its repeats.

Make a verse and a chorus

  1. Write the verse’s chords and melody.
  2. Open the Form panel, click Add Section and choose Chorus (or click Add a section in the Form row). The bars you already have become the Verse, and four empty bars are added after them as the Chorus.
  3. Write the chorus in its new bars. To make it longer, drag its block’s right edge in the Form row.

If your song already holds both the verse and the chorus, select the chorus’s notes or chords, right-click them (or touch and hold) and choose Make Section from Selection: their bars become a section of their own, and nothing moves. Choose Chorus in its menu. Make Section from Selection is also in Add Section, in the Form row’s + and, on a Mac, in the Edit menu. It is dimmed while nothing is selected, while the selection crosses into another section, or when its bars are already a section; on a Mac, hold the pointer over it to read why.

Repeat the chorus

  1. In the Form panel, open the chorus’s repeats (×1) and choose how many times it plays, from ×2 to ×4. Its block in the Form row shows it too.
  2. Turn Loop off and press Play: the chorus plays that many times, and its row and block count the pass as it plays (1/2, 2/2).

An exported song plays its repeats too. With Loop on, the loop plays the bars as written, once each. A repeat plays the same bars again; for a last chorus that changes, choose Duplicate instead and edit the copy.

Split a section at the playhead

  1. Click the ruler in the bar where the new section should start, to move the playhead there.
  2. Choose Split at Playhead in the section’s More menu (or its block’s menu). It splits at the start of that bar, and the second half is a section of the same kind, played once.
  3. Choose another kind for the second half, or Rename… it.

Split at Playhead is dimmed while the playhead is outside the section or in its first bar.

Give a section its own key or tempo

A section plays in the song’s key and tempo until you give it its own: a last chorus up a tone, a bridge at half the pace.

  1. Choose Key and Tempo… in the section’s More menu (or its block’s menu).
  2. Turn on Its own tempo and type or nudge the tempo, or turn on Its own key and choose the key and scale. Each starts from the song’s, so nothing changes until you change it.
  3. If the section already has notes or chords and the key moves, Chordling asks first. Transpose moves the section’s notes and chords into the new key; Keep the Music leaves them where they are and reads them in the new key, for a bridge you already wrote in its own key. An empty section changes at once.

The section’s block shows what it sets itself, as Em · 84. Everything follows it there: the click, the band, the chord names and suggestions, the melody grid’s scale, the sung parts, and the exported audio and MIDI. The song pill at the top reads what is in effect where the playhead is; its popover still sets the song’s key and tempo, and names the section the playhead is in when that section has its own. Turn both off to go back to the song’s. A section you move, copy or split keeps its own key and tempo, and each pass of a repeat plays them.

Move, resize, copy or delete a section

  • Move: drag its row in the Form panel, or drag its block along the Form row, where it lands where you drop its middle. Move Earlier and Move Later move it one place at a time.
  • Resize: drag a block’s right edge in the Form row, by whole bars. New bars are empty; the music in bars you take away leaves the song, and everything after it moves up.
  • Copy: Duplicate puts a copy right after the section, music and all. The two aren’t linked, so you can change either.
  • Delete: removes the section and the music that starts in it, and closes the gap. Deleting the only section left keeps the music and removes just the form.
  • Name: a section reads its kind, numbered when the song has more than one (Verse 1, Verse 2), until you Rename… it; an empty name goes back to the kind’s.
  • Select: click a block in the Form row to select the section, along with the notes of the lane you’re editing that start in it (or its chords, while the chords have the edit).

Keys in the Form panel (Mac): once you’ve clicked a row, ↑ and ↓ choose a section, Return moves the playhead to it, ⌥⌘↑ and ⌥⌘↓ move it, ⌘D duplicates it and Delete deletes it with its music. Click the notes again and Delete is theirs.

Give the bridge its own drums

The band’s drums follow the form on their own. Each section plays at its kind’s energy (Intro and Outro low, Chorus and Solo high, the others in between), with a fill into each new section, a crash at its start, the chorus on the ride cymbal and an ending on the last bar. To give one kind of section a groove of its own:

  1. Turn the drums on: pick a style with the Drums lane’s Band chip, or turn on Drums in its Groove chip.
  2. Click the Drums lane, or choose Edit Drums… at the foot of the Groove chip’s popover. The drum editor opens.
  3. Open the Whole song menu at its top and choose Bridge. The menu lists each kind of section your song has.
  4. Choose a groove from the Groove menu, or click steps in the grid to change the one there. The menu now reads Bridge · Own groove.
  5. If you like, set the bridge’s Energy (Auto, Low, Medium or High) and turn its End fill off. Press Play to loop the bridge, then Done.

A kind’s groove plays in every section of that kind, so all your choruses play alike. Follow the song, at the foot of the same menu, gives the bridge the song’s groove back, and Whole song edits the groove the other sections play. In the grid, click a step to turn it on and double-click it to clear it; drag up or down on a step to set how hard it plays, or sideways to paint a row. Click a row’s name to mute it, and + Row adds a drum the groove doesn’t play. While the song’s Fills is Off, End fill is off too.

The band

The band’s two lanes, Bass and Drums, sit under your parts in Arrange (in Write and Harmonize they’re chips in the Band row) and play along with your chords.

  • Band (the Drums lane’s second chip) picks a style, Rock trio, Bossa, Ballad, Jazz combo, Pop, Funk or Shuffle, that sets the drums, the bass and the chords’ rhythm at once; once you change any of them it reads Custom. Its popover also sets the chords’ Rhythm, from Hold to Kick + snare, and Arpeggio.
  • Groove (the Drums lane’s first chip) turns the drums on or off and sets their groove, their Kit (Acoustic, Bridged-T, Six-Bit or Hand Percussion), their Fills (Off, Sections, or Sections and phrases) and their Swing (Straight, Light, Medium or Heavy; a triplet groove already swings and has none). While the drums play they keep time in place of the metronome’s click; the count-in still clicks.
  • Sound (the Bass lane’s first chip) turns the bass on or off and picks Finger, Pick, Fretless or Synth. Line sets what it plays (Root, Root and fifth, Octaves or Walking) and its Feel: With the kick, With the chords, Every beat or Eighths.

The band’s level, mute and solo are in its first chip’s popover. On an iPhone, each band lane’s one chip opens all of its settings. The band and the drum editor come with Chordling Pro; until then their chips wear a lock, and choosing one shows what Pro holds.

Keys for arranging (Mac)

⌘3 opens Arrange. Space plays and pauses, ⌘. stops, Home goes back to the start and K turns the metronome on or off. ⌥↑ and ⌥↓ move the edit to the part above or below. ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo and redo, in the drum editor too, where Return is Done.

Settings

On Mac, Settings is its own window (Chordling › Settings…, ⌘,); on iPad and iPhone it’s in the top bar’s More menu. It holds iCloud, Appearance, Language, Notation (scale degrees or letter names, and sharps or flats), Pitch Range, Pitch Matching, Timeline Ruler, Metronome, Playback, Bed and Tone, and ends with About Chordling.

Export and Fretling

  • Export Audio writes the song as an audio file (CAF). On Mac it’s File › Export Audio… (⇧⌘E); on iPad and iPhone, More › Export Audio opens the share sheet.
  • Export Progression (File › Export Progression…, ⌥⌘E) saves the song’s chords as a file that Fretling, the sister app for guitar, opens on its fretboard. Import Progression… (⇧⌘I) brings a progression back.

Chordling Pro

Locked features wear a small lock and stay in sight. Tap one to see what Pro holds, its price, Try Pro free for 14 days and Restore Purchases. One purchase covers Mac, iPad and iPhone and can be shared with your family. Details are on Free & Pro.

Help

The microphone doesn’t work

If you turned the microphone down, Chordling shows Microphone Access Required with a button to the right settings. On iPad and iPhone, turn on Microphone in Settings › Chordling. On Mac, enable Chordling in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone. Then try again.

My songs don’t appear on another device

Open Settings › iCloud on both devices. Account should read Signed in to iCloud; if it reads Not signed in to iCloud, sign in to iCloud in the system settings. Use the same Apple Account on each device, and give a new song a moment to sync.

I bought Pro on another device

Open any locked feature and tap Restore Purchases, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with. A family member’s purchase reaches you through Family Sharing.

Something else

Write to adofito@gmail.com with your device, the app’s version (in About Chordling) and what happened.
