Guide and Support

Chordling keeps one song on one stage: the chords in the tall lane at the top, the melody right under them, and every other part in a slim lane below. The switch at the top picks what you’re doing: Write the chords and the melody, Harmonize to try chords for your melody, Arrange to set each part’s sound and level. This guide walks through each, then covers settings, exporting and getting help.

For Chordling 1.0 on Mac, iPad and iPhone. Menu commands are the Mac’s; on iPad and iPhone the same actions are on the lanes and in the top bar’s More menu (⋯).

Songs

Songs, at the left of the top bar, opens the song list, where every song lives. New Song opens a sheet with two ways in: Start with chords or Start with a melody. Start from Demo opens one of 20 demo songs, from a first scale to advanced harmony. Right-click or long-press a song to Rename or Delete it; deleting can’t be undone. There’s no limit on how many songs you keep.

The melody

The melody has its own lane, a piano roll under the chords. With the Draw tool, drag across the grid to place a note; the tinted rows are the notes in your key. The Tools menu on the melody lane switches between Draw, Select and Erase, and sets Snap to. To play a tune in instead, turn on Tap keys on the melody lane: a keyboard strip opens under the melody, and each key you tap adds the next note. Undo is in the top bar; Redo is in More.

The chords

The chords sit in the tall lane at the top. In an empty song the lane offers a Start from menu of ten progressions to begin with, such as Pop I–V–vi–IV, Jazz ii–V–I, the 12-bar blues and the Andalusian cadence; once there’s a melody it offers Chords for this tune too.

For the whole progression at once, tap the Chords lane’s Sound chip and choose Edit Progression… at the foot of its popover. The progression editor shows the chords four ways: Wheel, Map, Ladder and Voices. Its detail panel ends with Under the melody: the chords that contain the note your melody holds where the pick would land, and that note’s role in each.

Color bands and melody guides

Each chord’s color washes down its bars behind the melody, so you can see which notes fit. Color bands (Bands on an iPhone) on the melody lane turns it off or on. For finer guides, open the melody’s Part settings (at the foot of its lane’s popover, or More › Part settings) and choose a level under Melody guides: Scale, Chord Tones, Guide Tones or Targets. The Chords lane’s Part settings add Guides on the canvas, with chord guides and their strength.

The song’s form

The Form row above the ruler holds the song’s sections. Tap + to add one, then right-click or long-press it to set its kind (Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge, Solo, Outro), rename it, repeat it up to four times, duplicate it, split it at the playhead or move it.

Harmonize a melody

Choose Harmonize in the switch at the top (on a Mac also View › Harmonize, ⌘2). Rows A, B and C of chords appear under the song’s chords, and A fits your melody best. The bar above them sets the style: How rich runs from Simple and Sevenths through Colors and Dominants to Jazz; a Flavor (Pedal point, Modal or Minor color) plays in its place; and How often sets how often the chords change (Every bar, Every half bar or Follow the melody). The rows answer as soon as you change any of them.

Tap a chord in any row and that bar takes it into your mix, with why it is there under it; tap a row’s letter to take the whole row. Pick a different row for each bar and press Play to hear your mix in the song’s place. Harmonize again keeps the bars you picked and finds new chords around them. Apply mix (or Apply A for one row whole) writes the chords into your song, in one step you can undo.

Simple and Sevenths are free. Colors, Dominants, Jazz and the three flavors come with Chordling Pro.

Why it works

Why it works, on the Chords lane’s header, says what each change does and the cadence it makes, for the chord playing or the one you select, drawn as a map or a wheel. On an iPhone it’s at the foot of the Chords lane’s popover. Add chords first; it reads whatever the song holds.

Harmony parts

Add a part that harmonizes your melody with + Part, at the foot of the lane names (on Mac also Harmony › Add Harmony, ⇧⌘H). Add Harmony adds the blend Chordling suggests for your melody, and More blends… lets you pick another: Classic blend (below), Wide blend (below), Blend above, Octave double, Follow the chords or Bass under melody. Empty Part gives you a part to write by hand. Each part gets a slim lane with its sound, level, mute and solo, and its Part settings hold its name, color and blend.

One harmony part is free. A second and third part, and the sung Voice sounds, come with Chordling Pro. A song holds up to three harmony parts.

Listen and Pitch Matching

Listen, in the top bar’s More menu (on a Mac also Playback › Listen), shows the pitch you sing on the melody lane while it’s on. It’s free. The Pitch Matching Test (Settings › App › Pitch Matching › Start the Test…) grades how closely you match a sung or piano guide; it comes with Chordling Pro. Neither records anything: see the privacy policy.

The band

The band’s two lanes, Bass and Drums, sit under your parts. In Arrange, the Drums lane’s Band chip picks a style (Rock trio, Bossa, Ballad, Jazz combo) that sets the bass line, the drums and the keys’ rhythm at once; the Bass lane’s chips set its sound and line, and the Drums lane’s Groove its pattern. On an iPhone, each lane’s chip opens all of its settings. Each lane has its own level, mute and solo. The band comes with Chordling Pro.

Settings

On Mac, Settings is its own window (Chordling › Settings…, ⌘,); on iPad and iPhone it’s in the top bar’s More menu. It holds iCloud, Appearance, Language, Notation (scale degrees or letter names, and sharps or flats), Pitch Range, Pitch Matching, Timeline Ruler, Metronome, Playback, Bed and Tone, and ends with About Chordling.

Export and Fretling

  • Export Audio writes the song as an audio file (CAF). On Mac it’s File › Export Audio… (⇧⌘E); on iPad and iPhone, More › Export Audio opens the share sheet.
  • Export Progression (File › Export Progression…, ⌥⌘E) saves the song’s chords as a file that Fretling, the sister app for guitar, opens on its fretboard. Import Progression… (⇧⌘I) brings a progression back.

Chordling Pro

Locked features wear a small lock and stay in sight. Tap one to see what Pro holds, its price, Try Pro free for 14 days and Restore Purchases. One purchase covers Mac, iPad and iPhone and can be shared with your family. Details are on Free & Pro.

Help

The microphone doesn’t work

If you turned the microphone down, Chordling shows Microphone Access Required with a button to the right settings. On iPad and iPhone, turn on Microphone in Settings › Chordling. On Mac, enable Chordling in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone. Then try Listen again.

My songs don’t appear on another device

Open Settings › iCloud on both devices. Account should read Signed in to iCloud; if it reads Not signed in to iCloud, sign in to iCloud in the system settings. Use the same Apple Account on each device, and give a new song a moment to sync.

I bought Pro on another device

Open any locked feature and tap Restore Purchases, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with. A family member’s purchase reaches you through Family Sharing.

Something else

Write to adofito@gmail.com with your device, the app’s version (in About Chordling) and what happened.
